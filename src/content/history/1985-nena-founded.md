The Northeast Neighborhood Association (NENA) is Bozeman’s first neighborhood association. Its beginnings were borne out of problem solving and necessity.

NENA was officially formed in 2001 by volunteers who were applying for a grant to create the “pocket park” near the historic railroad depot. The park was part of a neighborhood strategy to oppose a garbage transfer station, which the City wanted to locate near the park. 

Previously, neighbors had successfully organized to oppose a bronze foundry that had been approved for the neighborhood. This opened the opportunity to rezone the block from M-1 (light industrial) to R-3 (residential) and to revise the 1990 Master Plan so that the future vision for the larger neighborhood would be residential instead of industrial.

NENA is also Bozeman’s largest neighborhood, extending from N. Broadway, Front Street, and the railroad to the east; North Grand Avenue to the west; Mendenhall Street to the south; and East Oak Street to the north.

NENA’s bylaws state that its mission is: “to promote and preserve the qualities of our uniquely diversified neighborhood that are important to the residents, businesses and city of Bozeman.” Its goals are: “to provide information to the membership about issues that affect the quality of life in the area; to take on proactive and participatory roles concerning the issues that affect the quality of life in the area; to take on an activist role when necessary to protect the quality of life in the neighborhood; and to attempt to create opportunities for our community to network, engage, and have some fun!”

Some of the fun includes regular neighborhood potlucks and the infamous annual “Parade of Sheds,” when NENA invites all to come experience the gardens, sheds, yard art, and general funkiness of the northeast neighborhood. The current struggle NENA faces is how to preserve that unique character, livability, and affordability in the face of rising land prices and development pressure.
