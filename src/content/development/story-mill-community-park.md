**Story Mill Community Park** is a 60-acre city park at 698 Bridger Drive, just east of the Northeast Neighborhood. NENA's east boundary is North Broadway, Front Street, and the railroad, so the park is outside the neighborhood. Neighbors still reach it on the trails from this side of town.

In 2006 a developer bought the property, including the mobile-home park on the site, for a project of 1,200 homes and 140,000 square feet of commercial space. [Trust for Public Land](https://www.tpl.org/stories/story-mill-park-bozeman-montana) writes that families living in the mobile homes were evicted, and that the 2008 recession ended the project when the bank foreclosed. The [Landscape Architecture Foundation case study](https://www.landscapeperformance.org/case-study-briefs/story-mill) calls that mobile-home park the Bridger View Trailer Park. It says many residents were displaced after buyouts, or were evicted when the park was abandoned because living conditions were unsafe. The same case study says Trust for Public Land acquired the property after the foreclosure. A [Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis article](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2022/what-works-in-housing-affordability-creating-middle-income-housing-with-the-bridger-view-neighborhood) on the later housing dates that purchase to 2012. That article calls the park Bridger Trailer Court and says 92 families lived there. Trust for Public Land says more than 100 families were evicted.

In the 2012 election, Bozeman voters passed a Trails, Open Space and Parks bond with 73 percent support. Trust for Public Land writes that the bond generated over $4 million in public funding for the park. The Landscape Architecture Foundation lists the project budget as $6.5 million and the completion date as summer 2019. Trust for Public Land says the park opened in 2019.

The case study describes a 40-acre nature preserve and a 20-acre area with play space, gardens, and trails. The City of Bozeman owns and operates the park. Eight acres at the edge were set aside for housing. That land is now the [Bridger View Neighborhood](/development/bridger-view-neighborhood).

More than half of the park lies in the [Northern Pacific / Story Mill Historic District](/history/1996-northern-pacific-story-mill-historic-district-added-to-the-national-register-of-historic-places), according to the case study. The historic Story Mill grain elevators stand across from the park.

## Why it matters to the neighborhood

The park is the open space at the east end of trails neighbors use, including the [Front Street pathway](/news/2021-12-10-front-street-connector-trail). NENA's [Trails & Parks](/our-work/trails-pocket-parks) work includes the connection from the neighborhood to Story Mill. The trailer park is part of the same story. People who lived there were displaced, and the [Bridger View](/development/bridger-view-neighborhood) homes are the housing that later returned to part of the land.
