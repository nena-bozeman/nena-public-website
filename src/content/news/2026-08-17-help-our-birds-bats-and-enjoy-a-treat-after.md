Join this year’s **[Burdock Blitz](/events/2026-08-22-burdock-blitz)** — **Saturday, August 22, 9:00 a.m.** on the patio between [Fink’s](/places/finks-deli) and [Biome](/places/biome-slow-craft).

For the past several years, NENA and Midtown neighbors have cut burdock, an invasive weed whose velcro-like seed balls can trap small birds and bats. The work is making a huge difference: every year the remaining weeds are fewer.

Bring pruning clippers or loppers if you have them, and wear gloves, long sleeves, and long pants of fabrics that burs won’t stick to.

We should be done before noon. Afterward, gather at [Roly Poly](/places/roly-poly-coffee-co) for a hot or cold coffee drink (donated by them) and a tasty bite provided by NENA.

Please RSVP to [delmue@yahoo.com](mailto:delmue@yahoo.com) so organizers know how many to expect for coffee and treats.

## Why this timing works

Only second-year burdock produces flowers and seed heads. Pruning now gets the seed balls before they drop, and late enough that plants can’t grow new flowers. First-year stalks get cut and can’t mature into next year’s seed producers.

Sacajawea Audubon members Paulette and Andy Epple started this project in problem areas. Commissioner Jen Madgic brought it to NENA and has participated alongside neighbors every year since — as have Midtown neighbors in their neighborhood.
