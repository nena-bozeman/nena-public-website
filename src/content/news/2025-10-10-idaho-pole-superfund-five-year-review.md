The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) reviewed the cleanup efforts (remedy) at the Idaho Pole Superfund site, where historic wood treating practices contaminated soil and groundwater. Because they placed restrictions on how the land can be used, EPA reviews the cleanup remedy every 5 years to see if it is protective of human health and the environment—or if additional work is needed.

You can read about the history of cleanup at the site on the [EPA website](https://cumulis.epa.gov/supercpad/cursites/csitinfo.cfm?id=0800379).

A developer (Scout DAC, LLC) bought a parcel of site property in 2022 and [plans to develop it](https://yorkdevelopments.com/projects/idaho-pole/).

According to EPA Remedial Project Manager, [Jessica Smith](mailto:Smith.Jessica.G@epa.gov), Scout DAC plans to submit a draft of the "Treatability Study Report" (showing the findings of contaminant results after treating soils last year) to EPA in mid-November 2025. A Master Site Plan outlining the development project (which includes the work plan for soil relocation work) will be submitted to the City of Bozeman in March 2026. They anticipate soil relocation work being completed late summer/early Fall 2026.

## Issues identified by EPA

1. There is no formal schedule for inspection of the treated soil areas (TSA) and woody vegetation may be growing on the edges of the treated soil areas.
2. New floodplain maps show some of the treated soils are in the floodplain.
3. 1-Methylnaphthalene is present in groundwater at concentrations that may result in unacceptable vapor intrusion risk if buildings were to ever be constructed near groundwater contaminated with 1-methylnaphthalene.

[Read the review online](https://semspub.epa.gov/src/document/08/2224913)

## Recommendations made by EPA

1. Prepare an operations and maintenance plan that identifies a formal schedule for inspection of the TSAs and how any identified deficiencies will be addressed.
2. Determine if additional remedial action is needed to protect the remedy based on new floodplain maps and to meet applicable or relevant and appropriate requirements (ARARs) for floodplains and solid waste disposal/management.
3. Conduct a vapor intrusion study to determine if vapor mitigation is necessary.

[Read the online fact sheet](https://semspub.epa.gov/src/document/08/100017938)
