Every August, NENA and Midtown neighbors cut **burdock**, an invasive weed whose velcro-like seed balls can trap small birds and bats. The work is making a difference: each year the remaining weeds are fewer.

Sacajawea Audubon members Paulette and Andy Epple started the project in problem areas. Commissioner Jen Madgic brought it to NENA and has participated alongside neighbors every year since.

## Why late August

Only second-year burdock produces flowers and seed heads. Cutting then gets the seed balls before they drop, and late enough that plants cannot grow new flowers. First-year stalks that get cut cannot mature into next year’s seed producers.

Bring pruning clippers or loppers if you have them, and wear gloves, long sleeves, and long pants of fabrics that burs will not stick to.
